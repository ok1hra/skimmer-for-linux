FT8 and FT4 beside CW, RTTY and WSPR: WSJT-X's own decoders, built in, on every FT8/FT4 frequency inside the bands received. Each station heard goes out on the telnet feed and, if you want, to PSK Reporter, and the waterfall gets an FT8 palette. FT8 is off until you turn it on; the CW and RTTY decoders and their feed lines are as in v0.8.9.

## FT8 and FT4

- **WSJT-X's decoders, not a lookalike:** the FT8 and FT4 decoders of WSJT-X 3.0.2 (Fortran), built in as `skimmer-ft8d` and driven as WSJT-X's `jt9` decodes from disk. On recorded real air (10 min of 20 m by day) they copied about 41 FT8 messages a cycle, down to −23 dB.
- **From the same IQ, no extra receiver:** every FT8/FT4 dial frequency of WSJT-X's list whose 200–3500 Hz above the dial lies inside a band received. At 96 kHz from each band's low edge that is FT8 on 160–10 m and FT4 on 80–17 m. `bands=` limits it to the bands named.
- **Every station that sends is a spot**, not only the CQ callers: once in 10 minutes per band and mode, at its own frequency (dial + its audio offset), e.g. `DX de OK1HRA-#: 7075.1 G3TKT FT8 -2 dB DE 2106Z`. **CQ** marks a CQ caller and **DE** any other station sending. Hashed calls `<…>` and a-priori decodes are never spotted, and `[filter]` applies.
- **The CW/RTTY feed is unchanged:** FT8 and FT4 lines go only to the local telnet feed, never to the RBN Aggregator's port (the RBN takes FT8 its own way). `feed=false` keeps them on the web page only.
- **To PSK Reporter** (`upload=true`) as WSJT-X reports, under your call and locator:
  - each station once an hour per band and mode, with its locator when it sent one;
  - a datagram every 5 minutes.
- **The load:** a busy band (FT8 + FT4, depth 2) costs about a fifth of a core. `depth` (1 Fast, 2 Normal, 3 Deep), `ft4=false` and `bands=` lower it.
- FT8 is off on a worker (its hub decodes the IQ) and on a band the thermal guard has paused.

Settings → **FT8 / FT4**: on, FT8, FT4, to the feed, depth, the audio window, bands, decoders, PSK Reporter. The tile's note lists the dials your bands take and how the decoder runs.

## On the web page

- **The FT8 palette:** *FT8* in the waterfall's bar, or a click on an FT8/FT4 spot, opens what the skimmer decodes on a dial, as WSJT-X's Band Activity: UTC, dB, DT, Hz and the message, cycle by cycle, CQs in green, and a click on a call marks its rows.
  - The waterfall narrows to the dial's window, a faint line marks each cycle and each station's call stands on its trace.
  - Closing the palette gives the view back.
- **The waterfall** marks the FT8/FT4 windows in violet (*FT8 RX*, *FT4 RX*), as it marks WSPR's in red.
- **Status:** a column *FT8 stations 30 min*, and FT8/FT4 in the continents' heat map.
- **The DX cluster:** FT8 and FT4 are in the mode filter. The signal filter now starts at −30 dB, because WSJT-X's SNR goes down to −24 dB; a stored −10, the old default, moves with it.

## Statistics

- **FT8 is a mode of its own** beside CW and RTTY, recorded per band:
  - its spots, in a chart of their own (there are far more of them);
  - the stations heard a minute, as a third strip in *active decoders by band*;
  - a row in the tables.
- **The CPU chart shows the helper programs:** the FT8 and WSPR decoders run beside the skimmer, and their CPU was not counted before.
- **The statistics file is version 6, about 32 MB (was 22).** It is carried over at the first start, and the version 5 file is kept beside it as `stats.bin.v5` for an older build.

## Install

A skimmer on v0.8.8 or later with `[update] mode=auto` (the default) installs this release by itself at its update hour; Settings → Updates → *Install now* does it at once. By hand:

```sh
mv skimmer-headless-0.8.10-x86_64.AppImage skimmer-headless-x86_64.AppImage
chmod +x skimmer-headless-x86_64.AppImage
./skimmer-headless-x86_64.AppImage
```

Building from source now needs `gfortran` for FT8/FT4. Without it the build leaves FT8 out.
