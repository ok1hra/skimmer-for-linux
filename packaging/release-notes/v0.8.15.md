JS8 joins CW, RTTY, FT8/FT4 and WSPR: decoded in all four submodes at once by JS8Call-improved's own decoder, and shown in every statistic, bar and table of the page. The page also gets a guided tour behind a **?** in the menu.

JS8 is off until you turn it on. CW, RTTY, FT8/FT4 and WSPR decode as in v0.8.14, byte for byte.

## JS8

Turn it on in Settings → JS8, or with `[js8] enabled=true`. `bands=` limits it to some bands, for example `bands=40m, 80m`.

- **Which frequencies:** every JS8 dial frequency of JS8Call's list (160 m to 6 m) that lies inside a band received. At 96 kHz from a band's low edge every band holds its JS8 frequency.
- **Every submode at once:** normal (15 s), fast (10 s), turbo (6 s) and slow (30 s, the deepest: down to −28 dB) are all decoded every period, from the same IQ. A skimmer cannot know which one a station uses.
- **The decoder:** JS8Call-improved's own decoder, built in as `skimmer-js8d`. One process per frequency keeps running, so it combines a frame repeated over several periods; that also costs a tenth of the CPU of one run per period. All four submodes take about 2.5 % of a core per band, measured on recorded 20, 40 and 30 m.
- **The spots:** a heartbeat, a CQ or a directed message names the station that sent it. Each station is a spot once per band in 10 minutes, at its own frequency, on the telnet feed as `JS8  -18 dB  CQ`, or `DE` for a directed message. It never goes to the RBN Aggregator's port. With `upload=true` the stations go to PSK Reporter under the FT8 / FT4 call and locator.
- **Deep frames wait for a confirmation:** JS8 carries only a 12-bit check, so a frame read with many errors could be a false call. Such a *deep* frame shows on the page at once, faded and marked "?". Its call goes to the telnet feed only once it is confirmed: a second decode within 30 minutes, MASTER.SCP, or a call already confirmed on JS8 in the last 90 days. `clean` (default 0.80) sets where deep starts.
- **On the page:** JS8 has its own colour (gold) in the bands table, the heat map of the continents, the DX cluster's Mode filter, the map, and Statistics. Statistics show the digital modes in a chart of their own, FT8/FT4 up and JS8 down. On the waterfall each JS8 frequency's window is marked, and the **JS8** button opens the JS8 palette.
- **The JS8 palette** shows what the frames say, put back together into messages as JS8Call shows them, including the compressed text: for example "F4LPU: DJ3EI INFO YAESY FT-891 (30W) …". It uses the JS8 protocol module and dictionary of [WIFILT](https://github.com/ok1hra/wifilt).

## The statistics file

Statistics now count JS8 as a fourth mode. The history file grows from about 32 to about 43 MB. Your history is kept: the old file is converted at the first start and saved beside it as `stats.bin.v6`, for an older version if you ever go back.

## A guided tour

A **?** in the menu on Status, Statistics and Waterfall starts a tour of that page. The page dims around one part at a time, and a card says what the part is and how to use it. *Got it* goes to the next part, *Back* goes back, and *End the tour* (or Esc) ends it at once.

## Fixes

- **A peer's FT8/FT4 spot on the RBN port:** a CQ that another skimmer sent in FT8 or FT4 came out on this skimmer's feed as a CW-style line, and went to the RBN Aggregator's port too. It is now a digital line on the telnet feed only, like this skimmer's own.

## Install

A skimmer on v0.8.8 or later with `[update] mode=auto` (the default) installs this release by itself at its update hour. Settings → Updates → *Install now* does it at once. By hand:

```sh
mv skimmer-headless-0.8.15-x86_64.AppImage skimmer-headless-x86_64.AppImage
chmod +x skimmer-headless-x86_64.AppImage
./skimmer-headless-x86_64.AppImage
```

Building from source now also needs a C++20 compiler and Boost's headers for JS8 (`g++ libboost1.81-dev` on Debian 12, `g++ libboost-dev` on Debian 13). Without them JS8 is left out.
