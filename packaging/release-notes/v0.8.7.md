WSPR is decoded beside CW and RTTY, from the same IQ, and can be sent to WSPRnet. RTTY spots open a decoder over the waterfall, and the waterfall, the map, the band settings and the thermal guard have grown. The decoder and the feed are as in v0.8.6.

## WSPR (Settings → WSPR)

- **Decoded from the IQ already received.** Every WSPR-2 frequency of WSJT-X's list whose passband (dial + 1500 ± 150 Hz) lies inside a received band is decoded at each even UTC minute. No extra receiver is needed. At 96 kHz, with bands from their low edge, that is 160, 80, 40, 30, 17 and 12 m. The decoder is WSJT-X 3.0.2's own `wsprd`, built in as `skimmer-wsprd`, so nothing else needs to be installed.
- **Sent to WSPRnet** with *send the spots to WSPRnet* on, under the WSPR call and locator. When these are empty, the station's own call and locator are used. A refused or unreachable server keeps the spots for the next cycle.
- **Deeper candidate search** (`deep`, wsprd `-d`, off by default) and **Subtract decoded signals** (`subtract`, on by default as in WSJT-X), as WSJT-X has them.
- **Where the stations show:**
  - Statistics lists the stations of the last 24 h: locator, distance, bands, last heard, SNR, best, power, decodes, drift. The list can be sorted.
  - Status → Bands has a column *WSPR stations 30 min* with a bar, and a link to [wspr.aprsinfo.com](https://wspr.aprsinfo.com).
  - The waterfall shows each WSPR passband dark red, marked *WSPR RX*.
- Off by default (`[wspr] enabled=false`).

## RTTY palette over the waterfall

A click on an RTTY spot (its speaker in the DX cluster, or its call over the waterfall) opens the waterfall tuned there. Over it opens the RTTY palette of WIFILT's QRPlog: the audio scope with its mark/space markers, two decoders, AFC and AUTOTUNE. It decodes the waterfall's audio in the browser. *RTTY* in the waterfall's bar opens and closes it. Settings → RTTY palette holds its defaults for every viewer (`[rtty_palette]`).

## The waterfall

- **Listen to a spot.** A speaker in the DX cluster opens the waterfall on the spot's band, listening there.
- **Tune with the wheel.** The wheel over the spectrum tunes in 10 to 100 Hz steps, finer when zoomed in; Shift gives 10 Hz steps. The frequency heard is a field in the bar: type a frequency and press Enter to tune there, on any band.
- **The calls near the cursor** show in a tip at the mouse.
- **The calls over the picture** are only those heard while it was drawn, so each name has its trace under it.
- The *RTTY* button is easier to see.

## The map

- **HEAT** shows where the spots come from as a heat map of the DXCCs, weighted by their spots and SNR.
- **DOTS** turns the spot dots off; each spot is then its line alone, with its call and SNR at the end.
- Where the spirals of crowded DXCCs overlap, their spots are lines only, with no blot of dots.
- The heading says the age chosen in the DX cluster's UTC filter, not a fixed 30 min.

## Bands (Settings → Bands)

- **Bands are picked as segments of the rate,** as CW Skimmer offers them: 45.5, 91 or 182 kHz from each band's low edge, from 160 to 6 m. Up to 8 segments can be on. The panel also shows the IQ data rate the radio sends.
- **From CW Skimmer** takes CW Skimmer Server's rate and ticked segments. It reads SkimSrv.ini from wine on the same PC, or from a file you choose. Only the band keys are read, never the operator's details.

## Thermal guard

- It now pauses a band when the 5-minute mean is over the limit now and for 3 of the last 5 minutes. A short dip under the limit no longer starts the count again. Before, a mean hovering at the limit could keep one band off for an hour and never pause the next.
- A paused mode reads **OFF** in the band tables, and a band with every mode paused is grey along its row.
- Statistics marks the pauses faint red in *active decoders by band*.

## Statistics and status

- **The decoders tile** counts the decoders reading text now, out of all those running. Paused bands are left out.
- **The spots chart** has a *smooth* switch: averaged, round areas instead of columns.
- **The table** reads left to right, each number before its bar, and every heading says over what range.
- **The three tables at the foot** (bands, WSPR, visitors) start folded. One you open stays open in your browser.
- **Visitors who signed in from outside** are listed under the table: the whole log for the LAN, only the calls of the last 24 h from outside.
- **Visitors from outside see the CPU temperature,** and so why the thermal guard paused a band. The CPU load and IQ lost stay hidden from them.

## Before you upgrade

**The statistics file is now version 5:** it also keeps the thermal guard's pauses. A v0.8.6 file is converted on the first start, and the old one is kept as `stats.bin.v4`. To go back to v0.8.6, put `stats.bin.v4` back as `stats.bin` (in `~/.local/state/skimmer-for-linux/`) first.

The new settings (`[wspr]`, `[rtty_palette]`) are optional, and v0.8.6 ignores them.
