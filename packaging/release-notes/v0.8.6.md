Four new features: visitors from the internet sign in with their call, the night at the station shows under the statistics, RTTY can decode during contests only, and a thermal guard pauses a band while the CPU runs hot. The decoder and the feed are as in v0.8.5.

## Visitors from outside the LAN

- **They sign in with a callsign.** Who comes from outside the local network sees the page, but no data until they enter their call: 3 to 15 letters, digits and `/`, with at least one digit and one letter. A wrong one is refused, and the window stays. The call is kept for 30 days in a signed cookie, so a returning visitor is not asked again. The server enforces it: without signing in, `status.json`, `stats.json` and `/api/spots` answer 401, and the waterfall stays closed.
- **Who need not sign in:** whoever may change the settings, and clients from `[visitors] free_from` (default: the private networks and loopback). A client that comes through a reverse proxy always counts as from outside.
- **A log of the visits** in `~/.local/state/skimmer-for-linux/visitors.tsv`: the call, the address, the time, how long, and the frequencies watched on the waterfall or listened to for at least 10 s. Failed sign-ins go to `visitors-failed.tsv`. The Log tab shows both.
- **The visitor's call is in the page header.** From another DXCC, they are a **star on the map**, and AUTO zoom takes them in.
- **No machine readings from outside.** The CPU, its temperature and IQ lost are left out of the header, the status and the statistics for anyone outside the LAN, signed in or not.
- **The telnet address in the header.** Settings → Station and feed → *Telnet address* (`[feed] address`): host:port, or a `telnet://` URL. Everyone sees it in the header as a link.
- Sign-in can be switched off in Settings → Visitors.

## Statistics

- **The grey line.** The charts are darker where it was night at the station, with soft dusk and dawn. The night comes from the locator (else the call's DXCC) and the date. On long ranges the nights blend into a seasonal shade instead of hundreds of stripes. A button in the toolbar turns it off.
- The temperature chart shows the thermal guard's limit as a dashed line.

## Adaptive RTTY (Settings → Decoding)

With `rtty_adaptive` on, the RTTY decoders run only during a contest. They switch on at each full UTC hour and at start for 2 minutes. If more than 100 RTTY spots come in on all bands, they stay on; otherwise they stop until the next hour. A paused mode takes no CPU, and the statistics show a gap rather than zero spots. Off by default.

## Thermal guard (Settings → Thermal guard)

- **When it acts.** The 5-minute mean of the hottest CPU sensor stays over the limit for 5 minutes. The default limit is the sensor's critical temperature − 15 °C (85 °C on coretemp), else 80 °C.
- **What it pauses.** The band with the fewest spots in the last 30 minutes. If it is still hot 10 minutes later, it pauses the next one. One band always stays.
- **The hysteresis.** The drop each pause brought is measured and kept as that band's hysteresis. A band comes back, the last paused first, only once the mean has stayed under the limit by that drop (5 °C at least) plus a margin for 20 minutes, so bands do not flap.
- The Status page shows the mean, the limit, what is paused and what each pause brought.
- Off by default.

## Before you upgrade

**Visitor sign-in is on by default.** A page published to the internet, or through a reverse proxy, now asks its visitors for a call. To keep the page open to all, untick Settings → Visitors → *visitors from outside sign in with their callsign* (`[visitors] login=false`).

The statistics file is as in v0.8.5, and the new settings are optional. Going back to v0.8.5 needs nothing; it ignores them.
