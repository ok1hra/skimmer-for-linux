# CQ WW CW 2012 — tuning v2 against CW Skimmer Server on the same air

A full-contest wideband recording (CQ WW CW 2012, six bands, 48 h) and the
spots CW Skimmer Server (VE3NEA) produced LIVE from the same receiver
(`spots.txt`, spotter OL5Q-#) let skimmer-for-linux be measured and tuned
offline against a strong reference. Tools: `skimmer-compare/cqww/`. Ideas
for decoder changes come from Dan's notes.

## The data and its two defects

- 15-minute WAV files per band, 16-bit stereo I/Q at 192 kHz, `auxi` chunk
  (start time + centre). `skimmer-replay` reads them from a file or stdin
  (`ssh host cat a.wav b.wav | skimmer-replay -`).
- **Q is sampled 0.46 sample ahead of I.** Every signal has a coherent image
  mirrored around the centre, only 11 dB down at 35 kHz and 2 dB at 87 kHz
  (the phase error grows with frequency). Without correction the decoder
  reads the mirrors as stations. `SKIM_REPLAY_IQ_SKEW=0.46` fixes it
  (windowed-sinc fractional delay); the value is the same on all bands and
  hours (fitted by minimising the f/−f coherence).
- **The recorder dropped blocks of 16 384 frames (85 ms) under load** — the
  files' frame counts fall short of `auxi` stop − start by 0.1 % at night
  to 32 % in daytime, on all bands at once. Every drop is a splice in time
  that tears CW apart (20 m 11Z, 13 % missing: L recall 14 % vs R 84 %).
  Splices of noise into noise are invisible in the samples, so they cannot
  be repaired reliably. **Only hours with ≤ 0.3 % missing are used:
  25 Nov 02Z and 03Z** (plus ~4 % at night elsewhere, not used).

## Referee and scoring (`score.py`)

- Unit: call × band × hour, all spots (CQ and S&P) — S&P spots only in the
  replay (`rbn_any_caller`); the live feed stays CQ-only.
- REAL call: sent a public log (all 6 899 CQ WW 2012 CW logs), worked by
  ≥ 2 logs, or spotted by ≥ 2 RBN skimmers other than OL5Q.
- SEEN: placed on that band near that time and frequency by an RBN spot
  (≤ 10 min, ≤ 1 kHz) or a log line (≤ 10 min, ≤ 1.5 kHz).
- TRUE = real and seen, or real with no rival. BUST = a RIVAL exists: a
  real look-alike (≤ 2 edits) within 300 Hz in the same band-hour that is
  seen or has more spot lines, beside at least half of the unit's lines
  (catches misreads that happen to be another real call: "K3C" beside
  K3CR). UNCONF otherwise. With this referee R busts 4–7 %, which matches
  live measurements of CW Skimmer Server.
- Recall is counted on U = TRUE units heard by R or the variant.
- Frequencies: L sits a constant +0.1 … +0.2 kHz above R's spots
  (centre convention); the scorer measures and removes the offset.

## Method

- Dev: 40 m + 80 m 25 Nov 02Z. Frozen test: 40 m + 80 m 03Z, 160 m 02Z +
  03Z; noise-only check: 10 m + 15 m 02Z (dead bands, R spotted nothing).
- `run.py` replays a band-hour as 4 × 15 min in parallel, each with 180 s
  of warm-up from the previous file. The replay runs on stream time, so
  speed does not change results, but **the decoder is chaotic**: two whole
  runs that differ only by 10 s of warm-up differ in 11 % of their units
  (aggregates within ~1 point). Split vs whole differs more (23–28 % of
  units, recall ~1 point lower: warm-up spots inside the hour are dropped
  and the feed's dedup then withholds the call). A/B pairs therefore always
  use the same mode; milestones use whole hours.
- `gate.py` re-runs only extractor + station table + feed gate from the
  replay's decode taps (seconds). `corpus.py` dumps the channelizer output
  of the channels where R placed calls and benchmarks the CW backend alone
  on them (`skimmer-cw-bench`, ~2 min): copy rate of R's TRUE calls.
- `breakdown.py` sorts the units R heard and L missed by what the decode
  log shows (silent / garbage / misread / read but not spotted) and the
  busts by Morse error type.

## What was found and changed

Defaults now: the validator fix, `SKIM_V2_EDGE` and the near-call gate
(`SKIM_GATE_NEAR=300`, `SKIM_GATE_NEAR_DICT=2`) are on; everything else
is a switch. "0" turns a default off.

| Change | Switch | Effect (dev) |
|---|---|---|
| D4C, C5A, P3N … (prefix + 1-letter suffix) refused by the validator; the extractor now also takes MASTER.SCP calls; T4 added; CALL/CALL | always on | +0.7 / +0.2 recall (40/80 m); 35 real calls, 1 299 R spots were unreachable |
| near-call conflict: a non-dictionary call waits while a look-alike within 300 Hz is in the dictionary or read more often; a dictionary call yields to one read 2× as often; non-dictionary calls settle 30 s | `SKIM_GATE_NEAR=300`, `SKIM_GATE_NEAR_DICT=2`, `SKIM_GATE_SETTLE_NODICT=30` | bust 26.5 → 5.1 % (40 m), 17.1 → 3.7 % (80 m), recall −1.3 / −0.6 |
| Rayleigh view fades with SNR — on a strong signal it counted element tails and gap QRM as mark, element spaces fell under the 0.7-dit ESP floor, dits fused into dahs (HG5C → "HGBC") | `SKIM_V2_RAYFADE=4,10` | 40 m recall 63.6 → 72.6 %, 80 m 79.4 → 79.7 % |
| edge model: marks on k·Tu + δ, spaces on k·Tu − δ, ESP floor under the space ideal | `SKIM_V2_EDGE=1` | with RAYFADE: corpus copy 59.0 → 60.1 %, 40 m recall +1.6 |
| invalid element sequence read as its unique one-element neighbour (Z35F keyed its 5 with six dits) | `SKIM_V2_NEAREST=1` | corpus copy +0.3 (not yet in the full pipeline) |
| carrier-centred 30 Hz low-pass ahead of the soft path's envelope | `SKIM_V2_NARROW=30` | corpus copy +1.8; full pipeline 40 m recall +0.2 but busts +1.6 — off |
| "<call> 5NN" in a runner's text marks the call (S&P stations named by the runner) | `SKIM_CALL_EXCH=1` | recall over the union ~90 % vs R 45–65 % — **a policy question, off** (see below) |

v2 also skips a log() per lattice candidate (memo of the
duration prior): byte-identical output, replay 2.15× faster.

### Why RAYFADE is not on by default

The Rayleigh view is also what carries a sub-dit flutter notch through a
dash (cw-test: 35 ms at -18 dB every 0.7 s, live-caught on 9A170NT). A
notch and a smeared element gap look the same sample by sample (~0.6 dit
at -10…-18 dB, above the noise), so the view cannot be faded for one and
kept for the other: with the fade cw-test splits the notched dashes
("MGA170NT"), without it real contest signals fuse dits. Tried and not
kept: a weight floor (0.15-0.35), a higher strict ESP floor (0.75-0.85),
µ_space learned up to 0.35 / 0.45 of the span, a fade keyed on how full
the committed element gaps are, a fade keyed on the measured edge smear
(dit - esp)/(dit + esp), and capping the view below the span midpoint.

### The exchange question

With `SKIM_CALL_EXCH=1` L reports the stations a runner works ("PR7HR 5NN
A4") at the runner's frequency. Callers zero-beat the runner, so the
frequency is about right, the public logs confirm the calls, and busts stay
at R's level (40 m 03Z: 4.4 vs 4.9 %), but it is a different inference from
what CW Skimmer does: it names a station from the other end of the QSO, not
from its own signal. It never reaches RBN (S&P spots stay local).

## Rejected (measured, not kept)

- Tone splitter (`SKIM_TONE_SPLIT=1`): dev recall −8 points.
- Narrow focus slot (`SKIM_TONE_FOCUS=1`): −12 … −17 points.
- Speed prior instead of the Schmitt bootstrap: corpus copy 60.1 → 51.5 %.
- Forgetting the fist after 20/60 s instead of 8: +0.0 … 0.3 (noise).
- `SKIM_GATE_SETTLE_NODICT=60`: busts lower but recall −2 … −3 points.
- Gate radius 150/500 Hz: same as 300; TTL 3 min: busts +3 points.

## Where the remaining 40 m gap is

Of the units R placed and L missed on 40 m 02Z (candidate), 82 of 116 are
S&P callers; 65 are misreads (a token within 2 edits is in the text), 42
garbage, 9 read but not spotted. Callers are short, often zero-beat in a
pileup, and the decode text around them is dominated by E/T fragments of
splatter. Character-level posteriors and coherent segment evidence (Dan's
notes) are the next candidates.
