Thermal guard Over the limit now and for 3 of the last 5 min (a dip under it does not start the count again): the band with the fewest spots in the last 30 min stops decoding (CW and RTTY); still hot 10 min later, the next — one band always stays. A paused band has no spots, so it is tested now and then: a band that opened takes the place of the quietest. The drop each pause brought is shown on Status.
Station and feed
Callsign filter
Without Regex, this matches plain text. With Regex enabled, it is a regular expression and ignores letter case.
characters off it
Compared place by place: a letter other, or missing at the end (a cut call), counts one change; the letters keep their places, so a call read backwards differs in all of them. These are the busts the skimmer makes of this station's own signal. A call MASTER.SCP knows passes all the same (a real neighbour), only my call itself never: for OK1HRA, two changes drop OK1HRB, OK1HR, OK1H, OK2HRB, while OK1BR and OK1RR (known) pass; 0 = the call itself. My call = Station and feed → Call. Only the calls that pass go anywhere; empty = all. The rest are dropped right where they are decoded: no spot on the telnet feed or to the RBN, no dot on the map, not in the DX cluster nor the statistics — peers' and workers' spots too.
Bad calls Prefixes or whole calls, separated by commas, any case. A prefix drops every call that starts with it (UA9 drops UA9ABC); a whole call drops that call, also with a /part (OK1ABC drops OK1ABC/P and DL/OK1ABC). They are dropped right where they are decoded, as by the callsign filter: no spot on the telnet feed or to the RBN, no dot on the map, not in the DX cluster nor the statistics — peers' and workers' spots too.

Reverse Beacon Network

The RBN takes spots only through its own program, the RBN Aggregator: the skimmer offers its spots on the port below and the Aggregator, connected to it, relays them to reversebeacon.net. Without a connected Aggregator no spot reaches the RBN (Status → "RBN now" counts who is connected). In the Aggregator: Skimmer IP = this machine, Skimmer Port = the port below, Skimmer Call = the call under Station and feed. One Aggregator serves one skimmer — beside CW Skimmer Server it takes a second Aggregator.

Status
RTTY palette Decodes in the browser (WIFILT's RTTY-ICOM decoder) what the waterfall's audio brings: 45.45 Bd, 170 Hz shift. RTTY in the waterfall's bar opens it too. Autotune moves the frequency heard once after each tuning, when a pair holds still; DEC 2 averages each bit and holds the rhythm. For everyone who watches the waterfall; CLEAN and REV in the palette are that browser's own.
WSPR WSPR-2 on WSJT-X's dial frequencies (USB, the signals 1400–1600 Hz above), each even UTC minute, WSJT-X's own decoder (wsprd) one band after the other — the radio's IQ, no extra receiver. A frequency counts when its 300 Hz lie inside 45 % of the rate around a band's centre (Bands below). The stations heard in the last 24 h: Statistics, under the bands' table.
FT8 / FT4 WSJT-X's own FT8 and FT4 decoders (built in) on its dial frequencies, every cycle — the radio's IQ, no extra receiver. Each station that sends (a CQ, a report, an RR73…) is a spot once a band and mode in 10 min at its own frequency, "FT8 -12 dB CQ" (DE for one not calling CQ) — never on the RBN Aggregator's port; hashed calls <…> and a-priori decodes never. PSK Reporter gets each station once an hour a band and mode, with its locator when it sent one, in a datagram every 5 minutes — as WSJT-X reports. FT8 in the waterfall's bar (or a click on an FT8 spot) opens the FT8 palette: every message of the last 15 min, as WSJT-X's Band Activity. Not on a worker (its hub decodes FT8); a band the thermal guard has off decodes no FT8 either.
JS8 JS8Call-improved's own JS8 decoder (built in) on JS8Call's dial frequencies, every submode every period — the radio's IQ, no extra receiver. A heartbeat, a CQ, a directed frame names its sender: a spot once a band in 10 min at its own frequency. JS8 has only a 12-bit check, so a frame read with many errors is deep: its call is on this page at once, on the telnet feed once confirmed — a second decode within 30 min, MASTER.SCP, or confirmed on JS8 in the last 90 days. The JS8 palette over the waterfall shows what the frames say.
Updates
A new version is checked against the sha256 GitHub publishes for it and run once on this config before it replaces the running AppImage; the old file stays beside it as ….AppImage.prev (Back to puts it back). A version that crashes three times right after the update goes back by itself (under the systemd service). Only a release AppImage replaces itself — a development build, or a program from a build tree, only reports. Name the file without a version (skimmer-headless-x86_64.AppImage): it keeps its name across updates.

The decoder talks to its board over SSH with its own key. Connect asks once for the board's root password (it is never stored) and puts the key there. Uninstall puts Pavel Demin's start back. Nothing changes on the board without one of these buttons.

About Skimmer for Linux
    
    

  

    
A CW, RTTY, FT8/FT4, JS8 and WSPR skimmer for the Red Pitaya, without a GUI: it decodes up to eight bands at once
      and sends what it hears to a telnet DX cluster feed, the RBN and this page.

    
Based on the original Skimmer
      for Linux by Richard Fakenberg, OK1BR, and running on the decoders of many authors:

    

      
CW v1, v2, v3 · RTTY
Richard Fakenberg OK1BR — the engine, the channelizer, callsign validation and the spot pipeline

      
CW v4 (DeepCW v2)
the narrow network and its stitcher by e04
        (deepcw-engine, MIT); its CPU kernel
        ported from SDRIAK by Vojtěch Bubník OK1IAK (GPL-3.0)

      
DeepCW v1
the model by e04 (deepcw-engine, AGPL-3.0 — downloaded on request, never shipped)

      
FT8 · FT4
WSJT-X by Joe Taylor K1JT,
        Steven Franke K9AN, Bill Somerville G4WJS and the WSJT-X team (GPL-3.0)

      
JS8
JS8Call-improved's
        decoder (the C++ port of JS8Call by Jordan Sherer KN4CRD, by the JS8Call-improved team, GPL-3.0), built without Qt as
        WIFILT builds it; the palette's frame reading
        and JSC dictionary from WIFILT by Dan OK1HRA

      
WSPR
wsprd of WSJT-X by Joe Taylor K1JT and Steven Franke K9AN, with Phil Karn KA9Q's Fano decoder (GPL-3.0)

Map · calls

        Natural Earth (public domain); MASTER.SCP (Super Check Partial)
