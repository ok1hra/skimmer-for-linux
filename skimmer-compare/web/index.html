How the two skimmers are compared
  • Both skimmers' spot feeds are recorded as they arrive. Only CQ spots are compared (the Linux skimmer spots nothing else), only inside the frequencies both receivers cover, and only while both feeds are up.
  • Each skimmer repeats its spots on its own schedule, so the spots of one call on one band are merged into an episode first. Same call, overlapping in time, close in frequency: both found it. Similar calls at the same place: one of them busted it.
  • Whether a call is real is decided by the Reverse Beacon Network — another skimmer spotted it (our own RBN spots never count) — and by the MASTER.SCP call list when RBN has nothing. Real stations are the stations at least one of the two skimmers found with a confirmed call.
  • Recall: the share of the real stations a skimmer found. Bust rate: the share of a skimmer's episodes whose call was wrong or that nobody could confirm. First spot: which skimmer spotted a station both found first.
  • A skimmer is called better only when the difference is unlikely to be chance (p < 0.05) and there is enough data; until then the verdict is undecided.

How to read this

Every real station of the session sits in one SNR column, by the signal-to-noise ratio the Windows skimmer reported for it. A station only the Linux skimmer heard is put on the same scale through the SNR difference of the stations both heard (— matched spots).

Top: the share of the column's real stations each skimmer spotted with the right call. Bottom, on its own scale: the share of each skimmer's episodes in the column with a wrong or unconfirmed call. The shaded band is the 95 % confidence interval — wide where a column holds only a few stations. Points resting on fewer than 5 stations or episodes are left out.

How to read this

The dot is the value over the whole session's compared time, the line its 95 % confidence interval (Wilson). Recall counts real stations found out of all real stations; the bust rate counts episodes with a wrong or unconfirmed call out of all the skimmer's episodes — a busted real station lowers both.

McNemar p: how likely a recall difference this large is by chance, from the stations only one of the two found. First spot W − L: the median of how much later W spotted a station both found than L (negative: W was first).

Data behind the numbers

The recordings behind the numbers above, for your own analysis. They are rebuilt every minute (the RBN file every 10 minutes) and cover the current session only.

    File formats
    • events.csv — one row per compared station (an episode: one call on one band): the time of its first and last spot, band and frequency, the kind (match, only L, only W, bust pair, W heard it not as CQ), whether it is a real station, the call each skimmer reported, who found and who busted it, the referee (rbn, scp, agree or none) with the number of RBN skimmers that spotted it, SNR on W's scale, WPM, and how much later W spotted it than L.
    • spots-L.tsv / spots-W.tsv — every spot line each skimmer sent, with the UTC time it was received. The Linux skimmer sends only CQ spots; the Windows skimmer's lines carry CQ, DE or no comment.
    • rbn.tsv.gz — the Reverse Beacon Network spots the referee consulted: the spots of other skimmers on the compared bands (our own RBN spots left out).
